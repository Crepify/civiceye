## 📄 LinkedIn Article — "The pothole that nobody fixed"

*Sources: Ministry of Road Transport & Highways (Road Accidents in India reports);
National Crime Records Bureau; Times of India / NDTV / Mathrubhumi reports on pothole
fatalities; ISWA / Lancet on waste-related disease burden.*

---

In October 2022, Uma Devi, 50, was riding pillion with her daughter on a Bengaluru road.
The two-wheeler lost balance trying to avoid a pothole. A bus ran over her. Her daughter
held the civic body responsible for her mother's death.

In 2026, Medha Akarsh — a mother of a three-year-old — was thrown from a two-wheeler on a
badly damaged stretch in Electronics City and run over by a goods vehicle. Residents
protested, demanding the road be fixed.

These are not isolated tragedies. They are a system's quiet, predictable output.

**The numbers are staggering.**
- In **2024 alone, 2,385 people died** in pothole-related road accidents in India — the
  highest ever recorded ([MoRTH via Mathrubhumi](https://english.mathrubhumi.com/news/india/pothole-deaths-surge-up-worst-hit-qpt2ii9k)).
- Between **2020 and 2024, potholes claimed 9,438 lives** across India
  ([Times of India](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/india/potholes-killed-9438-from-2020-to-2024-govt/articleshow/128279774.cms)) — up 53% in five years.
- Bengaluru has repeatedly topped the NCRB list for deaths "due to negligence of civic
  bodies" ([TOI](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/bengaluru/civic-neglect-bengaluru-has-most-deaths-by-pothole-related-accidents/articleshow/93936997.cms)).
- Karnataka alone: 24 deaths and 118 injuries from pothole-related accidents in 2024
  ([Bangalore Mirror](https://bangaloremirror.indiatimes.com/bangalore/civic/dodge-the-pits/articleshow/125093390.cms)).
- Across India, **172,000+ people died on roads in 2023** — one death every three minutes
  ([BBC](https://www.bbc.com/news/articles/c4g49pgx6d0o)). Road crashes kill more Indians
  every year than TB or malaria ([Down To Earth](https://www.downtoearth.org.in/governance/the-data-behind-indias-road-carnage-fatalities-soar-80-in-two-decades-despite-only-10-rise-in-accidents)).

And it's not just roads. Accumulated garbage breeds mosquitoes and disease — open drains
and waste dumps drive dengue, malaria and waterborne illness across India's cities
([India Water Portal](https://www.indiawaterportal.org/health-and-sanitation/health/open-drains-and-garbage-attract-dengue-spreading-mosquitoes-delhi-slums)). Globally,
an estimated 9 million deaths a year are linked to mismanaged waste and pollutants —
20× more than die from malaria ([WasteAid / ISWA](https://wasteaid.org/waste-climate-and-health/)).

**So why does nothing get fixed?**

Because nobody has the full picture. Complaints are scattered across helplines, apps and
WhatsApp groups that dissolve. A pothole is reported by twenty people who never know the
other nineteen exist. No single source says: *this is broken, this many of us reported it,
and this is who is responsible.*

**CivicEye changes that.**

- Photograph the problem — a pothole, a dead streetlight, an overflowing drain.
- Pin it to a map. AI identifies it, scores its severity, and flags unclear photos.
- Neighbours confirm it. Three confirmations → **Verified**.
- It lands, with photo, coordinates and priority, on the dashboard of the people whose
  job it is to fix it.

Not another complaint box. A shared, verifiable, living map of what a city needs.

We built it because we were once the people it's for: newcomers to a city, learning where
the hazards are by almost hitting them. Students. Freshers. Anyone who arrived somewhere
new and wished someone had said: *turn here — that road's dark.*

CivicEye is real. Reports, votes, verification, AI analysis, live detection, escalation
to authorities — a working system, not a mock-up.

A pothole will still be fixed one at a time. But now the city will know which one matters
most — because the people who live there said so.

**Making cities better, one report at a time.**

## ⚠️ Accuracy notes (important — keep these in the post or caption)

- All figures come from real, linked sources (MoRTH, NCRB, TOI, NDTV, Mathrubhumi, BBC,
  Bangalore Mirror, Down To Earth, WasteAid/ISWA, India Water Portal). Do not remove the
  links when posting the article — they make it credible.
- The 2024 pothole figure (2,385) and 5-year total (9,438) are India-wide. The 24 deaths /
  118 injuries are Karnataka 2024. The 2022 Uma Devi and 2026 Medha Akarsh stories are real,
  reported Bengaluru cases — keep the details accurate as written.
- "9 million deaths linked to mismanaged waste" is a global ISWA/WasteAid estimate, not
  India-specific — phrase it as "globally" to stay accurate.
- Garbage→disease is indirect (mosquito breeding → dengue/malaria, water contamination) —
  avoid saying "X people died of poisoned food from garbage"; use the accurate framing above.
